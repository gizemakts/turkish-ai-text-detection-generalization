# Calibration transfer: supplementary results and limitations

## Results

As a targeted post-hoc validation, we applied each existing inner-OOF-fitted Platt mapping and its unchanged nominal-FPR thresholds to independently held-out internal predictions generated by the corresponding final terminal checkpoint. Provenance, checkpoint hashes, method/seed/fold identities, outer-fold membership, and calibration/evaluation separation were verified for all 30 CE/SupCon realizations. CE terminal medians were Brier 0.0020, log loss 0.0104, and ECE 0.0026; mean observed FPRs were 1%: 0.0058, 2%: 0.0113, 3%: 0.0204, 5%: 0.0307. SupCon terminal medians were Brier 0.0021, log loss 0.0142, and ECE 0.0028; mean observed FPRs were 1%: 0.0051, 2%: 0.0111, 3%: 0.0187, 5%: 0.0293. Despite favorable held-out calibration and generally conservative internal FPRs, the 90th percentile absolute class-conditional terminal-versus-inner raw-score shift exceeded one pooled standard deviation for both methods, principally in AI scores; the resulting classification is therefore `CALIBRATION_TRANSFER_LIMITED`. This is descriptive rather than confirmatory: inner-fold and terminal models need not have identical score distributions, calibration intercept/slope was not estimable under complete separation in some realizations, repeated texts and folds are dependent, and internal operating-point behavior does not establish a mathematical or external-dataset FPR guarantee.

## Limitations

- The validation is post-hoc and uses the same internal corpus design, not a new population.
- Inner-OOF and outer-test observations differ in composition and difficulty.
- Terminal predictions are independent of mapping fitting within each outer split, but repeated across seeds.
- Exact binomial intervals are conditional on each fixed held-out fold and model.
- Score-distribution similarity is empirical compatibility evidence, not proof of exchangeability.
- No claim is made that nominal FPR transfers to external corpora.
